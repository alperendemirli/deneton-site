One system for everyone on the inspection.

Whoever owns the project sets it up and decides who does what. The workflow stays the same whichever side of the contract you're on.

Commissioning agents

Run the project for the client: set the stages, witness the tests, raise the snags and report progress.

Contractors

Run your own quality process, invite the witness when work is ready, and see exactly which snags are waiting on you.

Owners and clients

See where every asset stands without waiting for a weekly spreadsheet, and give each party the access it needs.

One loop, from the invitation to the close-out.

  1. Invite

    Send an invitation when an item is ready for inspection. The people who need to attend are notified.

  2. Witness

    Record the outcome, the progress and the photos on the spot. Unplanned inspections can be recorded without an invitation.

  3. Track

    The tracker shows every asset against every stage, so open, failed and upcoming work stays in view.

  4. Close out

    Raise snags against what failed, assign them to people or companies, and follow them until they are closed.

Records that hold up.

An inspection record is evidence, so Deneton treats it that way. Once a record is published it is locked. If something was wrong, a new record supersedes it and the original stays in the history.

  • Outcome, progress and photos on every record
  • Drafts for anything you need to finish later
  • The author can reopen a record within 30 minutes, and the reopening is logged

Snags that get closed.

A failed inspection becomes a snag with an owner. Assign it to people, to a company, or both, and everyone involved sees it move. Every change is kept, so nobody has to ask who changed what and when.

  • Your own snag types, statuses and priorities
  • Each company sees the snags assigned to it
  • Follow any snag and be notified when it changes

Access you design.

Roles aren't fixed job titles. Each project has its own roles, built from a tree of everything a person can see and do, so the setup matches the contract rather than the software.

  • Five starting roles, all editable
  • The same person can hold different roles on different projects
  • Buttons for things you can't do simply aren't there

Started on site.

Deneton was started in Istanbul in 2026 by a commissioning engineer who tracks inspections on large data centre projects every day, in spreadsheets and in general-purpose tools that were never made for the job.

It is built around how inspections actually run: the witness who has ten minutes at the equipment, the contractor who needs to know what's next, and the client who wants a straight answer on where things stand.
